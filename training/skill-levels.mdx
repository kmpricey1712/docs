Skill level is the single most important setting in Player Forged. It determines drill complexity, weekly volume, loading strategy, and whether features like Video Review and breaking-ball work are unlocked. The four tiers are built around **development stage**, not a birthday — a 13-year-old who has been playing seriously for three years may belong in Intermediate, while a 16-year-old just picking up the sport may need to start in Beginner.

<Note>
Age ranges are guidelines, not rules. Always choose the tier that honestly reflects your current skill and physical development. Starting too high risks injury and frustration; starting too low means you'll advance quickly and settle into the right phase within a week or two.
</Note>

## The four skill tiers

| Tier | Suggested Ages | Focus | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Beginner** | 7–10 | Fun, fundamentals, motor patterns | Low — short sessions, simple drills |
| **Developing** | 11–13 | Real technique, structured repetition | Moderate — consistent frequency |
| **Intermediate** | 14–18 | Intensity, progressive load, video review | Moderate-high — phase-based loading |
| **Advanced** | 18+ | High volume, periodization, refinement | High — full-week programming |

## Beginner (Ages 7–10)

Beginner is built for players who are just learning the game — or who need to reset and build a clean foundation. Every drill is designed to be enjoyable first and technically instructive second. You'll learn how to throw, catch, and move with athleticism before any position-specific demands are introduced.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="What you'll focus on" icon="star">
    Basic throwing and catching mechanics, introductory footwork patterns, and athletic movement fundamentals that apply to every position.
  </Card>
  <Card title="What's intentionally excluded" icon="ban">
    No heavy loads, no breaking pitches, no high-intensity conditioning. Arm care is built in gently — the program protects developing joints above all else.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

**Program design highlights:**
- Sessions are short (20–30 minutes) to match younger attention spans
- Drills use minimal equipment — a ball, a glove, and open space
- Milestones are achievable within a few weeks to build confidence and momentum
- Language in the app is accessible for younger athletes reading independently

<Tip>
Parents of younger athletes: Beginner programs are designed so you can supervise and participate without needing a coaching background. The drill descriptions explain the "why" in plain language.
</Tip>

## Developing (Ages 11–13)

Developing is where fundamentals become technique. Players at this tier have a basic feel for the game and are ready to build real, repeatable mechanics. The program introduces position-specific structure and adds enough volume to create genuine improvement over weeks of consistent work.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="What changes at this level" icon="arrow-up">
    Drills become more specific to your chosen position, session length increases, and the emphasis shifts from "try it and see" to deliberate repetition with a standard to meet.
  </Card>
  <Card title="What's still protected" icon="shield">
    Breaking-ball mechanics for pitchers are introduced carefully and only within pitch-count and effort guidelines. Overall load is still moderate to respect ongoing physical development.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

**Program design highlights:**
- Weekly frequency increases compared to Beginner
- Footwork and throwing mechanics are refined with specific checkpoints
- Self-review checklists become more detailed, building the habit of self-evaluation
- Phase milestones require more demonstrated consistency before advancement

## Intermediate (Ages 14–18)

Intermediate is where the program starts to feel like real training. Volume goes up, rest periods matter, and you're expected to push through sessions even when they're hard. This tier also unlocks **Video Review** for Pitcher and Hitter focuses, giving you objective feedback on your mechanics.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Key additions at this level" icon="dumbbell">
    Progressive load across phases, Video Review access, more complex drill variations, and conditioning work embedded within position-specific sessions.
  </Card>
  <Card title="What this tier assumes" icon="check">
    You already have functional mechanics and can execute the basics consistently. If technique breaks down under load, consider dropping back to Developing until it stabilizes.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

**Program design highlights:**
- Phase 2 drills add resistance, velocity intent, or constraint-based progressions
- Video Review enables side-by-side comparison of your mechanics across sessions
- Milestones require demonstrated performance output, not just completion
- Arm care for pitchers includes pitch-count awareness and recovery days

<Warning>
If you are 14–15 and early in your physical development, pay close attention to arm fatigue and joint soreness during pitching work. When in doubt, reduce frequency before reducing effort per session. Protect your arm — velocity comes later.
</Warning>

## Advanced (18+)

Advanced is for athletes who have mastered the fundamentals and are ready for high-volume, periodized programming. The program assumes you can self-manage recovery, identify technique breakdowns under fatigue, and push intensity without sacrificing form.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="What defines this tier" icon="trophy">
    Full-week programming across multiple sessions, periodization built into the phase structure, and an expectation of independent critical self-evaluation using Video Review and checklists.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Who should use it" icon="person-running">
    High school seniors, college players, independent league athletes, or any serious adult player who trains consistently and is pursuing genuine performance improvement.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

**Program design highlights:**
- Highest weekly volume of all four tiers
- Periodization concepts (accumulation → intensification) built into Phase 1 → Phase 2 structure
- Phase milestones reflect performance benchmarks, not just attendance
- Full Video Review integration with detailed checklist prompts for self-coaching

<Tip>
Advanced athletes: use the Speed & Conditioning focus as a complement to your position focus, not a replacement. Running your position program and your conditioning program in the same week — with intentional recovery days — is what high-level supplemental training looks like.
</Tip>

## Choosing honestly

<Accordion title="I'm unsure which tier to pick — how do I decide?">
Start by watching the Phase 1 drill descriptions for the tier you're considering. If the first phase feels like a review of things you already do automatically and correctly, move up a tier. If the very first drill asks you to do something you've never tried or can't execute consistently, move down a tier. You can always change your skill level — your progress in each focus at each level is tracked separately.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I change my skill level after I've started?">
Yes, at any time. Switching levels does not erase your history — your completed sessions and checklist entries remain logged. You simply start at Phase 1 of the new level when you switch.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="My coach assigned a level that's different from what I'd pick. Should I override it?">
Follow your coach's assignment. Coaches often set skill levels based on what they've observed over a full season — that context is valuable. If you genuinely believe the level is wrong for your development, talk to your coach directly.
</Accordion>
