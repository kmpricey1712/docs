---

Pitching development is a long game built on obsessive attention to mechanics, honest self-assessment through video, and a throwing program that earns velocity by first earning control. Whether you are stepping onto a mound for the first time or chasing another tick on the radar gun, every rep you throw should have a purpose. Player Forged structures your work the same way elite development programs do — layered progression, slow-motion feedback, and smart load management so your arm stays healthy for the long haul.

<Warning>
  Never use weighted balls or throw breaking pitches at the Beginner level. The arm structures of developing pitchers are vulnerable to the torque and load these drills produce. Weighted balls should only be introduced at the Intermediate level and beyond, and only with proper coach oversight.
</Warning>

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Beginner">
    At this level your entire focus is arm health and athletic movement. You are teaching your body how to throw, not how to pitch. Keep every session low-stress, stay loose, and prioritize feeling over outcome.

    <Note>
      No breaking balls. No weighted balls. No full-effort throws. Every drill here is designed to build the movement patterns your arm will rely on for years.
    </Note>

    <Accordion title="Step-and-Throw Catch">
      A foundational drill that connects your lower half to your throw. This is the starting point for every movement pattern you'll build later.

      **Steps**
      1. Stand 30–40 feet from a partner or rebounder with your feet together.
      2. Pick up your stride-side foot and step directly toward your target.
      3. As your foot lands, begin rotating your hips and let your arm follow naturally.
      4. Release the ball and finish with your throwing arm crossing your body.
      5. Do 10–15 reps, focusing on feeling the hip-to-shoulder sequence.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Step directly toward the target, not sideways
      - [ ] Hips rotate before the shoulder opens
      - [ ] Arm follows the body, not the other way around
      - [ ] Finish in a balanced, athletic position
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Big Target Catch">
      Reduces the pressure of accuracy so you can focus purely on your throwing motion and arm path.

      **Steps**
      1. Set up a large target — a strike zone mat, a taped square on a fence, or a partner holding a big mitt at roughly chest height.
      2. Stand at 45–50 feet.
      3. Throw easy, conversational-effort tosses at the center of the target.
      4. After each throw, check in: did your arm feel smooth? Did you stay balanced?
      5. Complete 3 sets of 10 throws with a short rest between each set.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Effort level stays at 50–60% — this is not a velocity drill
      - [ ] Eyes stay on the center of the target through release
      - [ ] Grip is relaxed, not white-knuckled
      - [ ] Arm path feels repeatable throw to throw
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Long Toss Lite">
      Begins to build arm strength and endurance through gradual distance, without pushing the arm beyond what it can safely handle.

      **Steps**
      1. Start at 30 feet and throw easy, flat-trajectory tosses to a partner.
      2. Every 3–4 throws, each partner takes one step back.
      3. Cap your distance at 90 feet. Do not push past this distance as a beginner.
      4. Once you reach 90 feet, work back in to 45 feet and throw 5–10 easy pulldown tosses.
      5. Session total should not exceed 20–25 throws.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Never throw harder than the distance demands — let the arc do the work
      - [ ] Stop immediately if you feel any elbow or shoulder discomfort
      - [ ] Keep a crow-hop before each throw to stay athletic
      - [ ] Rest at least one full day before your next throwing session
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Multi-Sport Throwing">
      Cross-training with different sports builds athletic arm action in a low-stakes environment.

      **Steps**
      1. Use a football, a frisbee, or a soccer throw-in to mix up your session.
      2. Throw a football with a partner at 20–30 yards for 10–15 reps — focus on the spiral release.
      3. Toss a frisbee back and forth, working on wrist snap and follow-through.
      4. Finish with 10 easy baseball tosses to reconnect the athletic feeling to your actual throw.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] You are training movement patterns, not stats — have fun with it
      - [ ] Notice how each implement forces a slightly different release and follow-through
      - [ ] Keep total arm stress low — multi-sport work counts toward your daily throw count
    </Accordion>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Developing">
    You now have healthy movement patterns. The developing level adds command basics — learning to throw the ball where you intend, developing a slow-motion self-review habit, and expanding your long toss distance responsibly.

    <Note>
      Begin recording your bullpen sessions from a side angle. You do not need to analyze every throw — even watching two or three at slow speed will start building your mechanical awareness.
    </Note>

    <Accordion title="Slow-Motion Self-Review">
      Video feedback is one of the most powerful development tools available to you. This drill establishes the habit.

      **Steps**
      1. Set up your phone on a tripod or lean it against a fence at hip height, angled from your glove-side (the side your arm arm extends toward the target).
      2. Record a set of 10 throws at your normal distance.
      3. Replay the video and scrub to slow motion at two checkpoints: foot strike and release point.
      4. Ask yourself: Are my hips fully rotating before my arm comes through? Is my release point consistent throw to throw?
      5. Write down one thing you want to feel differently on your next set, then throw again.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Camera is at hip height, not eye level
      - [ ] You are checking the same two checkpoints every session
      - [ ] You write your observation down — even a one-word note
      - [ ] You throw again after watching, not just observe
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Down-the-Middle Target Work">
      Establishes a command baseline by demanding consistent location to a single, central target.

      **Steps**
      1. Set up a strike zone target or ask your partner to hold a mitt at the middle of the zone.
      2. Throw from 45 feet. As your accuracy improves, push out to 54 feet.
      3. Throw 5 sets of 6 pitches. Your goal is 4 of 6 in the target each set.
      4. Track your hit rate each session in the Player Forged app.
      5. Once you hit 4 of 6 consistently over three sessions, move to the Two-Target Split drill.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Use a consistent grip — four-seam fastball only at this level
      - [ ] Reset between each pitch rather than rushing into the next
      - [ ] If you miss, feel where the miss was before throwing again
      - [ ] Track your numbers — progress becomes visible over weeks, not days
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Two-Target Split">
      Introduces the concept of intentional location by splitting the target into two sides.

      **Steps**
      1. Place two gloves or markers on the left and right edges of the strike zone at the knees.
      2. Stand at 45–54 feet.
      3. Call your target out loud before each pitch — either "glove side" or "arm side."
      4. Throw 10 pitches, alternating or mixing targets as you feel ready.
      5. Grade yourself: how many went to the intended side?

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Call the target before you throw — commitment is part of the drill
      - [ ] Focus on feel at release, not just on where the ball ended up
      - [ ] Misses are data, not failure — note which side you miss more
      - [ ] Keep effort at 70–75% so mechanics stay clean
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Long Toss Progression">
      Builds arm strength and loosens mechanical restrictions as you increase distance gradually.

      **Steps**
      1. Begin at 45 feet with 5 easy flat tosses to warm up.
      2. Every 4 throws, take one step back. Allow your arc to increase naturally.
      3. Target distance is 120–150 feet. Do not force distance — let the arm decide.
      4. Once you reach your max comfortable distance, work back in by taking two steps forward every 3 throws.
      5. Finish with 8–10 flat-seam pulldowns at 60 feet.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Never cross the arm — keep throws over the top or 3/4
      - [ ] Arc is your friend on the way out; flat is your friend on the way in
      - [ ] Crow-hop on every throw to keep the lower half engaged
      - [ ] Total throws including warmup: 40–50 max
    </Accordion>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Intermediate">
    At this level you are following a structured Driveline-style throwing program, working the corners of the strike zone with precision, and using video as a regular diagnostic tool. Arm care is still a priority, but you can now begin introducing weighted ball work under appropriate supervision.

    <Warning>
      Weighted ball training at the Intermediate level must be introduced gradually and only with guidance from a qualified pitching coach. Do not use plyo balls or overload balls without a defined program. Never throw weighted balls at high intent without a proper warmup sequence.
    </Warning>

    <Accordion title="Driveline-Style Daily Program">
      A structured block of throwing work that mirrors how elite development programs are organized.

      **Steps**
      1. **Arm Care (10 min):** Band work — W's, Y's, pull-aparts, and external rotations, 2 sets of 15 each.
      2. **Warmup (5 min):** 15–20 easy flat tosses at 30–45 feet.
      3. **Plyo / Connection Work (5 min):** If you have plyo balls, roll-ins and pivot pickoffs at 30–40 feet.
      4. **Command Block (15 min):** See Big-Target Corner Work and 4-Corners drill below.
      5. **Long Toss Block (10 min):** See Long Toss Ladder below.
      6. **Pulldowns / Recovery (5 min):** 8–10 high-intent flat tosses on the way back in, then light bands.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Never skip the arm care block
      - [ ] Log total throw count including warmup
      - [ ] Rest at least one full day between high-intent sessions
      - [ ] Record at least one command block session per week
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Big-Target Corner Work">
      Trains your eyes and feel to work the edges of the zone while maintaining the clean mechanics you have built.

      **Steps**
      1. Set up four targets on the corners of the strike zone — inside/outside at the knees and inside/outside at the letters.
      2. Stand at 54–60 feet (full mound distance if available).
      3. Call your corner before each pitch. Throw 4-seam fastballs only.
      4. Throw 5 reps to each corner for a block of 20, then rest.
      5. Track which corners you miss most and add 5 extra reps to those locations next session.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Commit to the corner before you wind up
      - [ ] Notice if you drift toward the middle under fatigue — that's data
      - [ ] Use your video review after this block at least once per week
      - [ ] Effort sits at 75–80% — accuracy, not velocity, is the goal here
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Long Toss Ladder">
      Progressively builds arm strength and trains maximal distance with athletic mechanics.

      **Steps**
      1. Warm up at 45 feet, then step back at defined intervals: 60 ft → 90 ft → 120 ft → 150 ft → 180 ft.
      2. Throw 3 reps at each rung of the ladder before stepping back.
      3. Stop at your natural max — do not throw through shoulder fatigue to reach a number.
      4. Work back down the ladder at the same intervals.
      5. Finish with 8 pulldowns at 60 feet, increasing effort to 85–90% on the final 3.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Crow-hop on every throw
      - [ ] Arc is intentional at long distances — not a flat heave
      - [ ] Pulldown effort is deliberate, not casual
      - [ ] Log your max comfortable distance each session and track it over months
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="4-Corners Command Drill">
      Sharpens your ability to locate to all four corners under light competitive pressure.

      **Steps**
      1. Number the four corners of the strike zone 1–4.
      2. Have a partner or your phone timer call a random corner every 10 seconds.
      3. Wind up and throw to the called corner on each rep.
      4. Throw 20 reps per set. Score yourself — one point per corner hit.
      5. Goal: 14 of 20. When you hit 16+ consistently, reduce the target size.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Commit immediately when the corner is called — no hesitation
      - [ ] Stay relaxed between reps; tension ruins command
      - [ ] If accuracy breaks down late in the set, note your throw count — fatigue is a teacher
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Video Mechanics Check-In">
      A structured weekly review to catch mechanical drift before it becomes a habit.

      **Steps**
      1. Record your bullpen session from two angles: glove-side and directly behind.
      2. Watch at 0.25x speed. Check these four points in order: balance point, hip rotation timing, release point height, and follow-through path.
      3. Compare to your previous session's clip — is your release point in the same spot?
      4. Write down one mechanical cue you want to apply at your next session.
      5. Share with your coach or a trusted training partner if possible.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Both camera angles every session — one angle is not enough
      - [ ] Check the same four points in the same order each time
      - [ ] One cue only — trying to fix three things at once fixes nothing
      - [ ] Archive your clips so you can track changes over months
    </Accordion>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Advanced">
    Advanced pitching development means every session has a purpose and every bullpen is a competition. You are using plyo balls in your warmup, working high-intent velocity bullpens, and pushing your long toss to maximum distance. Recovery and arm maintenance are non-negotiable at this level.

    <Note>
      At the advanced level, intent matters as much as mechanics. You should be training to compete, not just to practice. Every bullpen has an objective — command, velocity, or pitch mix — and you track it.
    </Note>

    <Accordion title="Plyo-Ball Warmup Sequence">
      A dynamic warmup protocol using plyo balls to activate the arm's rotational chain before throwing.

      **Steps**
      1. Start with 10 wall bounces at 6 feet — two-hand chest pass into a rebounder or solid wall.
      2. Move to pivot pickoffs: stand 20 feet from the wall, pivot from set position, and throw a plyo ball into the wall 8 times.
      3. Roll-ins: standing at 30 feet, take a 3-step run-up and throw a plyo ball into the wall 6 times, focusing on hip-to-shoulder sequence.
      4. Reverse throws: standing with your back to the wall, face away and throw overhead 6 times.
      5. Transition immediately to your regular warmup tosses at 45–60 feet.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Plyo ball warmup replaces, not supplements, band work on throwing days
      - [ ] Never max-effort plyo throws without the full sequence as a prerequisite
      - [ ] The wall must be a solid concrete or brick surface — soft walls absorb force instead of returning it
      - [ ] Listen to your arm — any sharp discomfort means stop immediately
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Mound Command Bullpen">
      A full-intent command session from the mound with defined objectives per pitch.

      **Steps**
      1. Set up a catcher or a strike zone target at regulation distance.
      2. Throw a 25-pitch bullpen with the following structure: 8 fastballs (2 to each corner + 4 with called location), 6 secondary pitches (offspeed/breaking), 6 fastballs at 85–90% intent to corners, 5 finish pitches simulating a 2-strike at-bat.
      3. Score each pitch: plus-one for location hit, zero for close miss, minus-one for wild miss.
      4. Target score: +10 or better out of a possible +25.
      5. Log your score and pitch-by-pitch notes in the Player Forged app.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Every pitch has a called location before you take the sign
      - [ ] Your catcher (or partner) gives honest feedback on each pitch
      - [ ] Velocity is secondary to location discipline in this bullpen
      - [ ] Review your video from this session within 24 hours
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Max Long Toss">
      Trains absolute arm distance and high-intent throwing mechanics.

      **Steps**
      1. Complete your full plyo warmup sequence and a 10-minute light toss.
      2. Extend to 90 ft → 120 ft → 150 ft → 180 ft → 210 ft → your max.
      3. At max distance, throw 4–6 reps with full crow-hop and maximum athletic intent — not just heaving, but driving through the throw.
      4. Work back down the ladder: 180 ft → 150 ft → 120 ft → 90 ft.
      5. Finish with 10 pulldown throws at 60 feet, ramping to full intent on the final 3.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Max long toss is done once per week maximum — it is a high-stress session
      - [ ] Pulldowns are the most important part — don't skip them
      - [ ] Log your max distance and how your arm felt at the end
      - [ ] At least 48 hours rest before your next mound session
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Velocity Intent Bullpens">
      Dedicated sessions where the objective is maximum velo with clean mechanics — not command.

      **Steps**
      1. Complete plyo warmup and a 15-minute progressive toss including pulldowns.
      2. Throw from the mound at 95–100% intent. Target: 15 pitches total.
      3. Take 3–4 pitches to build up. Throw the next 10–12 at full intent with a 30-second rest between each.
      4. Your catcher (or a radar app) tracks velocity on each pitch.
      5. Stop if your mechanics break down noticeably — bad-mechanic high-intent is injury territory.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Do not throw a velocity bullpen the day before or after a game
      - [ ] Radar readings are data, not ego — log them without attachment
      - [ ] If velo drops more than 3–4 mph from your peak during the session, stop
      - [ ] Recovery protocol: arm care bands and ice within 20 minutes of finishing
    </Accordion>
  </Tab>
