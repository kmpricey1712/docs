---
title: "Infielder Training: Footwork, Range, and Fielding"
sidebarTitle: "Infielder"
description: "Master the daily ground-ball routine, automatic footwork, and low-and-soft fielding fundamentals that define elite infield defense — Arenado-style."
---

The greatest infielders in the game share one trait: their footwork is so deeply rehearsed that it disappears. When a ball is hit hard to their left, they do not think about their first step — they are already moving. That automaticity is not talent. It is the product of thousands of daily reps, most of them ordinary and unglamorous, done with honest attention to posture, hand position, and foot timing. Nolan Arenado did not become one of the best third basemen in history by waiting for game day. He built his defense in practice, every day, at full intensity. Player Forged brings that philosophy to your at-home training — daily ground balls, honest posture checks, and progressive footwork that builds from basic readiness to automatic, game-speed execution.

<Note>
  Stay low. If there is one non-negotiable fundamental for infield defense, this is it. A player who fields from a low, wide base can adjust to hops and make difficult plays look routine. A player who stands up early will be beaten by even ordinary ground balls.
</Note>

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Beginner">
    At the beginner level you are learning what the ready position actually feels like in your body, developing your first footwork patterns, and getting comfortable fielding basic ground balls. Every rep should be slow enough that you can feel the correct position.

    <Accordion title="Ready-Position Ground Balls">
      Trains the fielding stance and the basic receive movement before any footwork or throwing is added.

      **Steps**
      1. Stand in your ready position: feet slightly wider than shoulder-width, knees bent, weight on the balls of your feet, back flat, glove out in front.
      2. Have a partner roll a slow ground ball directly at you from 20 feet away.
      3. As the ball approaches, bend your knees further and lower your glove to the ground — glove fingers pointing down.
      4. Field the ball with two hands, securing it in your throwing grip.
      5. Complete 3 sets of 10 rolls, resetting to the ready position between each rep.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Glove reaches the ground before the ball arrives — not at the same time
      - [ ] Two hands close together — bare hand is above the glove, not beside it
      - [ ] Back stays flat, not rounded — bend from the knees, not the waist
      - [ ] Eyes stay on the ball all the way into the glove
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Footwork Ladder Game">
      Uses an agility ladder to build the quick, precise foot patterns infielders need to move efficiently in all directions.

      **Steps**
      1. Lay an agility ladder flat on the ground (or use chalk lines as a substitute).
      2. Practice three patterns: **Two-feet in:** both feet touch each rung. **Lateral shuffle:** sideways through the ladder, one foot in each rung. **In-out:** both feet step in, then both step out to the side.
      3. Do each pattern 5 times at a slow, deliberate pace before increasing speed.
      4. Finish with 3 speed runs through your best pattern.
      5. Rest 30 seconds between each run.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Stay on the balls of your feet throughout — no flat-footed plodding
      - [ ] Keep your knees slightly bent even during fast runs
      - [ ] Arms swing naturally for balance — don't lock them at your sides
      - [ ] Accuracy of foot placement comes before speed — master the pattern first
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Roll-to-the-Side Fielding">
      Introduces lateral movement to ground balls, teaching you to move your feet to the ball rather than reaching for it.

      **Steps**
      1. Set up in your ready position. Partner rolls a ball to your glove side, 3–5 feet off your hip.
      2. Open your hips toward the ball (lead with the glove-side foot) and move laterally to get in front of it.
      3. Field the ball with two hands in your fielding position — do not reach across your body.
      4. Stand and reset. Repeat to your throwing-hand side.
      5. Complete 5 reps each side per set, 3 sets. Alternate sides every rep.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Move your feet first — never field a ball you could have gotten in front of
      - [ ] Stay in a low, bent-knee position during the lateral move
      - [ ] Glove stays out in front as you move, not down at your thigh
      - [ ] Field the ball at the center of your body, not off to one side
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Wall-Reaction Drill">
      Builds reaction time and hand-eye coordination by using an unpredictable rebound surface.

      **Steps**
      1. Stand 6–8 feet from a solid wall or rebounder in your ready position.
      2. Throw a tennis ball or rubber practice ball against the wall at varying heights and angles.
      3. React to the rebound and field it cleanly with two hands.
      4. Mix up your throws: high, low, left, right, directly at you. Do not set up the same bounce twice in a row.
      5. Complete 3 sets of 15 reactions. Rest 30 seconds between sets.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Start in a true ready position before each throw — not standing upright
      - [ ] Watch the ball off the wall, not your hands
      - [ ] React with your feet first, then your hands
      - [ ] Challenge yourself: the more unpredictable your throws, the better your reaction training
    </Accordion>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Developing">
    You now have the basic fielding stance and footwork. The developing level builds a daily ground-ball routine, introduces the transfer from field to throw, and begins working angles on balls hit to either side.

    <Accordion title="Daily Ground-Ball Routine">
      The backbone of infield development. Consistency over time beats any single flashy drill.

      **Steps**
      1. Have a partner hit or roll 30 ground balls to you in the following sequence: 10 directly at you, 10 to your glove side (moderate angle), 10 to your throwing-hand side (moderate angle).
      2. Field each ball cleanly, make a transfer, and simulate a throw to first (no actual throw needed at this stage — simulate the footwork and release point).
      3. Reset to your ready position between every single rep.
      4. Do this routine 4–5 days per week. Thirty balls takes under 15 minutes.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Every rep starts in a real ready position — not a lazy stand
      - [ ] Field and transfer happen in one flowing motion — no pause between them
      - [ ] Track the days you complete the routine in your Player Forged log
      - [ ] Increase to 40 balls per day once 30 feels comfortable over two weeks
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Transfer Basics">
      Trains the hand exchange from your glove to your throwing hand so it becomes a single fluid movement.

      **Steps**
      1. Stand still without a ball. Simulate the transfer repeatedly: both hands come together in front of your body, bare hand closes on the imaginary ball in the glove, wrist cocks, and elbow comes up to a throw position.
      2. Repeat 15 times dry, feeling the sequence.
      3. Add a ball: have a partner flip you a ball underhand. Catch it in your glove with two hands and execute the transfer immediately.
      4. After the transfer, make a short throw to a target 30–40 feet away.
      5. Complete 3 sets of 10 transfer throws.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Transfer happens at the front of your body, not at your hip
      - [ ] Bare hand closes on the ball — do not dig it out
      - [ ] After the transfer, elbow leads upward before arm extends forward
      - [ ] Practice the dry transfer daily — even just 10 reps at home with no partner
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Angle Work">
      Builds the habit of moving at the correct angle to field balls hit in the gap or at a sharp angle to either side.

      **Steps**
      1. Set up cones or markers at 45-degree angles to your left and right, 15–20 feet away.
      2. Partner rolls balls toward the cones at varying speeds. Your goal is to move on the correct angle to intercept the ball before it passes the cone.
      3. As you move, stay low and keep your glove out front. Do not just reach — get your body there.
      4. After fielding, plant and simulate a throw back to your partner's direction.
      5. Complete 5 reps each direction per set, 3 sets.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Plant the foot closest to the ball as your anchor, then move
      - [ ] Do not run upright — stay in an athletic bend while moving
      - [ ] Field the ball in your power zone — out front, low to the ground
      - [ ] Practice at slow speed until the angle feels instinctive, then add pace
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Wall-Reaction Drill (Harder)">
      Increases the difficulty of the beginner wall drill by shortening distance and mixing ball speed.

      **Steps**
      1. Move to 4–5 feet from the wall (closer than at the beginner level).
      2. Throw the ball against the wall harder and more randomly — off-center throws, near the floor, aimed high.
      3. React and field every rep. Two-hand the easy ones; challenge yourself to field one-handed on the toughest.
      4. Introduce a second ball: while fielding the rebound, your partner rolls a second ball to your side simultaneously.
      5. Complete 3 sets of 20 reactions.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Feet are always moving — you should never catch a ball flat-footed
      - [ ] The harder the drill, the more important your ready position becomes between reps
      - [ ] Two-ball variation requires a partner — do not skip it; it is the best reaction trainer available
    </Accordion>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Intermediate">
    Intermediate infielders are doing high-volume daily ground balls with intentional variation, developing exchange speed under time pressure, and beginning to work advanced plays like the pivot or the charge-and-throw.

    <Accordion title="High-Volume Daily Ground Balls — Varying Hops">
      Trains your ability to handle all three types of hops: the big hop, the short hop, and the dreaded in-between hop.

      **Steps**
      1. Have a partner hit or roll 50 ground balls per session using three distinct speeds: slow (big hop), medium (in-between), and hard (short hop).
      2. Segment the session: 15 slow balls, 15 hard balls, and 20 medium balls (the hardest). Mix freely in the final 10.
      3. For every rep, identify the hop type as you move: say "big," "short," or "in-between" out loud as you react.
      4. Field the short hop aggressively — move your feet to make it into a short hop if it is in-between.
      5. Track your clean-field percentage for the in-between hops specifically over multiple sessions.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Verbalizing the hop type builds the anticipation you need in games
      - [ ] On the in-between hop, attack it — do not back up and guess
      - [ ] Glove always reaches the ground on big hops — never gets lazy
      - [ ] 50 balls takes under 20 minutes; this is a non-negotiable daily habit
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Transfer and Exchange Speed">
      Builds maximum efficiency in the hand exchange from glove to throw, timed and tracked for accountability.

      **Steps**
      1. Set up your phone to film from a front angle or use the Player Forged timer.
      2. Have a partner hit ground balls to you. On each field, make a transfer and throw as quickly as possible while maintaining accuracy.
      3. Measure from field to release on video review. Target: under 0.5 seconds transfer.
      4. Complete 4 sets of 8 transfer throws. On the last set, attempt max-speed exchanges.
      5. Review video after each session: look for the specific point in the transfer where you lose time.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Speed comes from eliminating wasted motion, not from rushing
      - [ ] Both hands always come together at the front of your body — no hip transfers
      - [ ] Your throw must be accurate — speed without accuracy costs outs, not saves them
      - [ ] Log your transfer time per session and track the trend over weeks
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Pivot or Charge-and-Throw">
      Develops two advanced plays specific to your position: the double-play pivot (middle infield) or the charge-and-throw (corner infield).

      **Steps — Pivot (Second Base/Shortstop):**
      1. Set up at the bag with a partner at first base distance.
      2. Partner rolls a ball toward second. You field it, touch the bag with your foot, and throw to a first-base target.
      3. Practice three pivot footwork options: the inside pivot, the outside pivot, and the swipe-and-clear. Use the one that suits your position.
      4. Do 5 reps of each footwork option per session.

      **Steps — Charge-and-Throw (Third Base/First Base):**
      1. Partner places a slow roller 20 feet in front of you.
      2. Break toward the ball at full speed. Field it on the run with your glove hand.
      3. Plant your throwing-side foot and make a quick throw to a first-base target.
      4. Do 8 charge reps per session, mixing ball placement.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Pivot: touch the bag, don't step on it — drag your foot across to clear runners
      - [ ] Charge: read the ball's spin before committing to the final approach step
      - [ ] Both plays require full-speed practice — half-effort reps build half-effort habits
      - [ ] Video these from a side angle at least once per week to check footwork timing
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Video Posture Check">
      Uses slow-motion video to identify and correct the most common infield mechanics error: standing up early.

      **Steps**
      1. Set up your phone on a tripod at a 45-degree side angle, at knee height.
      2. Record 10 ground balls at your normal training speed.
      3. Watch at 0.25x speed. At the moment the ball enters your glove, freeze the frame. Check: are your knees still bent? Is your back flat? Are your eyes still down?
      4. If you are standing up before the ball is secure, this is your primary correction. Work 10 more reps with an exaggerated low hold and re-record.
      5. Do this check once per week until your low position is automatic.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Camera at knee height — eye-level video misses the posture errors
      - [ ] Check the exact moment of field contact, not before or after
      - [ ] If your back is rounded, you are bending from the waist — work on knee bend instead
      - [ ] One correction per session. Fix one thing fully before moving to the next.
    </Accordion>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Advanced">
    Advanced infielders train at full speed every day, tackle the hardest plays — the in-between hop and the slow roller — and stay ahead of the physical demands of their position with dedicated hip and lower-back mobility work.

    <Note>
      The daily routine at the advanced level is non-negotiable. Elite infielders do not take days off from ground balls. If you are short on time, do 20 at full speed rather than 50 at half-speed. Quality and intent matter more than volume when volume is limited.
    </Note>

    <Accordion title="Daily Routine at Full Speed">
      A high-volume, high-intensity daily ground-ball block that maintains and sharpens every fundamental.

      **Steps**
      1. Begin with 5 minutes of dynamic warmup: leg swings, lateral shuffles, hip circles.
      2. Field 60 ground balls in four blocks: 15 straight, 15 to the glove side (full range), 15 to the throwing-hand side (full range), 15 mixed at full-speed intensity.
      3. Every rep includes a full transfer and throw to a target. No simulated throws — all live.
      4. Maintain full intensity on every rep. If you feel yourself coasting on the 50th ball, you are not training — you are just going through motions.
      5. Log rep count and whether you stayed fully engaged throughout.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Full ready position before every single ball — no exceptions on rep 60 any more than on rep 1
      - [ ] Every transfer is sharp — no lazy hand exchanges in the back half of the session
      - [ ] If a ball gets through, note the type and add it to your next session's emphasis
      - [ ] Done 5–6 days per week. This is the daily habit that separates advanced infielders.
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="In-Between Hop and Slow-Roller Work">
      Dedicated practice on the two most difficult plays in infield defense.

      **Steps — In-Between Hop:**
      1. Partner hits medium-speed ground balls to you from 40 feet at a distance that naturally produces in-between hops.
      2. Your job: attack the ball aggressively with your first step so you convert the in-between into a short hop.
      3. Do not wait for the hop — go get it. 20 reps per session.

      **Steps — Slow Roller:**
      1. Partner places the ball 10–15 feet in front of you and calls "go."
      2. Charge at full speed. Field on the run — either barehand or with a short underhand scoop.
      3. Plant and make a quick, off-balance throw to first without setting your feet fully.
      4. Do 10 slow-roller reps per session. Work from both sides of the infield if possible.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] In-between hop: your decision to attack must be made before your third step
      - [ ] Slow roller: judge whether to barehand based on ball spin and your momentum — no hesitation
      - [ ] Both plays require full-speed practice — slow-motion reps build the wrong patterns
      - [ ] Video your slow-roller throws occasionally — off-balance mechanics are hard to self-diagnose
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Transfer Under Fatigue">
      Tests the durability of your exchange mechanics when your body is physically stressed — replicating late-game conditions.

      **Steps**
      1. Complete 10 minutes of conditioning before this drill: shuffle-step lateral slides (10 each direction), 20 bodyweight squats, and 10 broad jumps.
      2. Without rest, field 20 ground balls at full speed with live throws to a target.
      3. Record each rep. Measure transfer time from video.
      4. Compare to your rested baseline. Your goal: no more than 0.1 seconds slower under fatigue.
      5. Note which reps showed mechanical breakdown — these are your conditioning targets.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Do not skip the conditioning block — it is the whole point of the drill
      - [ ] Mechanics under fatigue must mirror mechanics when fresh
      - [ ] If footwork breaks down before hand speed, your legs need more conditioning work
      - [ ] Log your fatigue transfer time monthly and track improvement over the season
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Hip and Lower-Back Mobility Routine">
      A targeted recovery and maintenance circuit for the muscle groups most stressed by infield play.

      **Steps**
      1. **Lateral Hip Stretch (2 min):** Lie on your back, cross one ankle over the opposite knee, and pull the uncrossed leg toward your chest. Hold 30 seconds each side, 2 rounds.
      2. **Glute Bridge (2 min):** Lie on your back, feet flat, drive hips up and hold for 2 seconds. 3 sets of 12.
      3. **World's Greatest Stretch (3 min):** Step into a lunge, place the same-side hand on the ground inside your front foot, rotate the opposite arm to the ceiling. 5 reps each side.
      4. **Cat-Cow (1 min):** On all fours, alternate arching and rounding your back. 10 reps slow.
      5. **Seated Lower-Back Rotation (2 min):** Sit cross-legged, place one hand behind your head, rotate your elbow toward the ceiling. 10 reps each side.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Do this routine post-session while your muscles are warm
      - [ ] At least 3 times per week during the season — daily during heavy training blocks
      - [ ] Flag any persistent hip tightness or lower-back pain to a coach or trainer immediately
      - [ ] This is recovery, not a workout — move slowly and breathe through each stretch
    </Accordion>
  </Tab>
