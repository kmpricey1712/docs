description: "Build the quiet hands, low stable frame, and repeatable footwork that define elite catchers — from your first blocking drill to game-simulation reps."
---

Catching is the most physically demanding position on the field and one of the least understood. Great catchers are not just receivers — they are field generals who steal strikes, prevent stolen bases, and block balls in the dirt that keep runners from scoring. The foundation of all of it is deceptively simple: quiet hands, a low and stable frame, and movements so well-rehearsed they become automatic under pressure. Player Forged builds those patterns level by level, starting with the basics and progressing to full game-simulation reps that replicate real in-game stress.

<Note>
  Catcher-specific soreness is real. Your knees, hips, and lower back absorb significant load every session. Build recovery and mobility work into your schedule from day one — it is as important as the catching drills themselves.
</Note>

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Beginner">
    At the beginner level you are learning what "quiet hands" actually feels like, establishing your receiving setup, and getting comfortable receiving thrown balls and blocking them in a controlled environment. Keep every session short and focused.

    <Accordion title="Quiet Hands Wall Drill">
      Trains your hands to receive the ball softly rather than stab at it — the single most important habit a young catcher can build.

      **Steps**
      1. Stand 4–5 feet from a wall with your catcher's mitt on.
      2. Gently toss a tennis ball or soft training ball against the wall at chest height.
      3. As the ball comes back, receive it with your mitt — focus on letting the mitt give slightly with the ball, pulling it in rather than punching at it.
      4. Repeat 20 times. Then toss the ball at slightly different heights and angles: up, down, left, right.
      5. Finish with 10 two-hand receives, using your bare hand to secure the ball just after the mitt makes contact.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Mitt gives with the ball — no rigid stabs
      - [ ] Eyes stay on the ball all the way into the glove
      - [ ] Bare hand stays tucked away until after the mitt catches the ball
      - [ ] Shoulders stay relaxed and low throughout
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Setup and Target Drill">
      Builds a consistent, low receiving stance with a steady target for the pitcher.

      **Steps**
      1. Set up in your stance behind home plate (or a mat): feet wider than shoulder-width, weight forward on the balls of your feet, butt slightly above parallel.
      2. Hold your mitt out as a target — thumb-up position, glove in the lower half of the zone.
      3. Have a partner or parent softly toss a ball to your glove. Receive it and reset to the same position.
      4. Hold the target for 3 full seconds between every receive — stillness is the habit.
      5. Complete 3 sets of 10 reps, varying the toss location slightly each set.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Target is set before the toss, not just as it arrives
      - [ ] Stance resets to exactly the same position every rep
      - [ ] Weight stays forward — no sitting back on your heels
      - [ ] Mitt stays steady; avoid drifting to meet the ball early
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Soft-Toss Blocking Intro">
      Introduces the blocking position and teaches you to stay in front of the ball without panicking or flinching.

      **Steps**
      1. Start in your receiving stance. A partner kneels 8–10 feet away.
      2. Partner softly rolls or underhand-tosses a tennis ball to the dirt directly in front of you.
      3. Drop your knees to the ground simultaneously, tuck your chin to your chest, and round your shoulders forward so your chest and body create a wall.
      4. Let the ball hit your chest protector and drop in front of you. Do not try to catch it.
      5. After the ball drops, cover it with your mitt. Do 3 sets of 8 blocks.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Drop straight down, do not dive forward
      - [ ] Chin stays tucked — this protects your throat guard and keeps your eyes on the ball
      - [ ] Glove goes between your knees, blocking the gap
      - [ ] After the block, always locate the ball immediately
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Easy Catch-and-Toss">
      Develops the basic rhythm of receiving a pitch and making a soft, controlled throw back to the pitcher.

      **Steps**
      1. Crouch in your stance. A partner throws easy, lobbed pitches to you from 30–40 feet.
      2. Receive the pitch, come to a standing position, and make a controlled overhand throw back.
      3. Focus on footwork: right foot steps toward your partner as you release.
      4. Keep your throws easy — the goal is a smooth, consistent return, not a laser.
      5. Complete 20 exchanges per session.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Receive first, then stand — don't rush to throw before the ball is secure
      - [ ] Step toward your target before releasing
      - [ ] Throw stays at 50–60% effort
      - [ ] Reset to your full crouch between every receive
    </Accordion>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Developing">
    You are now building blocking range (not just straight blocking), learning the basics of pitch framing, and developing speed on your transfer from receive to throw. Consistency is the word of this level.

    <Accordion title="Blocking Progression — Side Balls">
      Extends your blocking ability to pitches in the dirt to the left and right, where most wild pitches actually land.

      **Steps**
      1. Set up in your stance. A partner stands 20 feet away with a bucket of tennis balls.
      2. Partner calls "left" or "right" just before tossing a ball to the dirt on that side.
      3. React by driving the knee on the ball's side to the ground first, then dropping the other knee, creating an angled wall.
      4. Your body should face the ball's direction — do not just reach with the glove.
      5. Complete 5 reps left, 5 reps right, 5 reps straight, rest, repeat for 3 sets.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Drive to the side, don't dive — one knee leads the drop
      - [ ] Shoulder on the ball-side drops to reduce the gap
      - [ ] Glove always seals between the knees
      - [ ] After the block, know where the ball is — verbalize "got it" in practice
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Framing Basics">
      Trains the subtle receive-and-hold technique that helps pitchers earn borderline strike calls.

      **Steps**
      1. Set up behind the plate. Partner throws easy pitches to four locations: the edges of the zone — inside corner, outside corner, top, bottom.
      2. As you receive each pitch, absorb it softly and hold the glove still for 1–2 seconds before returning it.
      3. For pitches on the edges, practice receiving the ball and holding your glove position rather than drifting back toward your body.
      4. Do not jerk the ball into the zone — the hold is subtle. Focus on the glove not moving after contact.
      5. Complete 5 reps at each edge location per set, 3 sets.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Receive the ball, don't stab it — quiet hands first
      - [ ] Hold the position for a full second after each catch
      - [ ] Avoid twisting your wrist dramatically — framing is about stillness, not manipulation
      - [ ] Practice in front of a mirror occasionally to see what a catcher or umpire sees
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Transfer Speed Drill">
      Builds the habit of a quick, consistent grip change from receive to throw so your pop time can develop naturally.

      **Steps**
      1. Crouch in your stance. Place 10 balls in a bucket next to you.
      2. Pick up a ball with your mitt hand, then immediately practice your transfer: bare hand closes on the ball in the glove, wrist cocks, elbow up to throwing position.
      3. Repeat this receive-to-grip motion 20 times standing still.
      4. Now add a partner throw: receive a toss, make the transfer, simulate a throw (no actual throw yet) back toward second base.
      5. Once the motion feels fluid, make actual throws to a target. Complete 10–15 transfers with live throws.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Bare hand meets the ball in the glove — do not pull the ball out separately
      - [ ] Transfer happens in one smooth motion, not two
      - [ ] Elbow punches upward first, then forward — same as a regular overhand throw
      - [ ] Time yourself from receive to release with the app's built-in timer
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Target Consistency Drill">
      Builds the habit of presenting a steady, reliable target on every single pitch.

      **Steps**
      1. Set up in your full crouch behind the plate.
      2. Before each pitch, verbally call a location — "low and away" or "up and in" — and move your mitt to that spot.
      3. Hold the target for a 3-count, then have your partner throw to you.
      4. Receive the pitch and note: did your glove stay in the called location, or did it drift?
      5. Complete 20 pitches per set. Mark which pitches caused your target to move before the throw arrived.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Target is set and held before the pitcher starts their motion
      - [ ] Mitt does not creep, drift, or drop while waiting for the pitch
      - [ ] Reset fully between pitches — no lazy half-stances
      - [ ] Track your drift rate over sessions — it should decrease week over week
    </Accordion>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Intermediate">
    Intermediate catchers are developing footwork that enables quick, accurate throws to second base, tracking their pop time, and reacting to mixed pitch types. Sessions become more athletic and game-realistic at this level.

    <Warning>
      Pop-time throw-downs put real stress on your throwing arm and shoulder. Warm up fully before any max-effort throw-down work and treat this as a high-intensity session that requires a rest day afterward.
    </Warning>

    <Accordion title="Footwork-to-Throw Progression">
      Develops the exact footwork pattern that drives efficient, accurate throws to second base.

      **Steps**
      1. Start in your crouch without a ball. Practice your footwork alone: receive (simulate), right foot pivots and steps at 45 degrees toward second, left foot follows and plants, arm comes through.
      2. Repeat the footwork 10 times dry — no throw. Feel the hip rotation driving the arm.
      3. Add a receive from a partner's throw. Receive, pivot, throw to a target at second base (or a cone at 127 feet).
      4. Focus on foot timing: right foot should be moving as the ball hits your glove — not after.
      5. Complete 3 sets of 8 live footwork-to-throw reps.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Right foot begins moving as the ball arrives — not after you secure it
      - [ ] Pivot drives your hips toward second base before the arm follows
      - [ ] Throw stays on a line — not a looping arc
      - [ ] Finish with your chest facing second base after each throw
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Timed Transfer Reps">
      Adds accountability to your transfer drill by tracking the time from mitt contact to ball release.

      **Steps**
      1. Set up your phone camera to record from a side angle, or use the Player Forged timer.
      2. Have a partner throw 15 pitches at varying locations — inside, outside, low, high.
      3. On each pitch, transfer and throw to second base (or a target) as quickly as possible.
      4. Review your recordings: measure from the frame the ball hits your glove to the frame it leaves your hand.
      5. Target: under 0.8 seconds transfer. Elite: 0.6 seconds or less.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Speed without accuracy is useless — track both in your log
      - [ ] Note which pitch locations slow your transfer most
      - [ ] Do not sacrifice footwork for hand speed — they work together
      - [ ] Rest 30 seconds between each throw to keep mechanics clean
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Pop-Time Throw-Down">
      A full-speed simulation of throwing out a base-stealer, measuring your total time from pitch catch to ball arrival at second.

      **Steps**
      1. Set up with a partner pitching from 45–60 feet and a second partner or target at second base (127 feet from home plate).
      2. Start in your full crouch.
      3. Receive the pitch and execute your full footwork-to-throw sequence at maximum intent.
      4. Time from when the ball hits your glove to when it arrives at second base — this is your pop time.
      5. Complete 6–8 throw-downs per session. Target pop time: under 2.0 seconds. Strong: 1.9 seconds. Elite: 1.8 and under.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Fully warmed up before your first max-effort throw-down
      - [ ] Do not sacrifice transfer quality chasing a clock number
      - [ ] Log every pop time reading in the Player Forged app
      - [ ] Treat this session as a hard throwing day — 48-hour recovery before your next
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Mixed-Pitch Reaction Drill">
      Trains your ability to identify pitch type at release and react appropriately — receive, block, or adjust.

      **Steps**
      1. Have a partner throw from 30–40 feet, mixing three pitch types: one that stays up for a receive, one in the dirt for a block, and one to the side for a diving block.
      2. Do NOT call the pitch type ahead of time — you react to where the ball is going.
      3. On a pitch in the dirt, block it. On a pitch to the side, block it. On a strike, receive and frame it.
      4. Complete 5 rounds of 9 pitches (3 of each type, mixed randomly).
      5. Grade yourself: did you make the correct decision on each pitch? Did you execute it cleanly?

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Commit to the decision — half-blocks are worse than full blocks
      - [ ] Stay loose between pitches so you can react without tension
      - [ ] Log which pitch type causes you the most decision errors
      - [ ] Increase pace when you can correctly identify all 9 cleanly
    </Accordion>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Advanced">
    Advanced catchers are preparing for game conditions — fatigue, high-stress situations, and the need to perform blocking, framing, and throwing consistently late in games. Sessions at this level simulate real game scenarios and include dedicated recovery protocols.

    <Note>
      Recovery and mobility work for catchers is not optional at the advanced level. Your knees, hips, and lower back accumulate significant stress over a season. Schedule a dedicated 15-minute mobility routine at least three times per week in addition to your skill sessions.
    </Note>

    <Accordion title="Game-Situation Blocking and Framing">
      Simulates in-game pressure by combining blocking and framing decisions within a live at-bat scenario.

      **Steps**
      1. Set up with a full battery — pitcher throwing from the mound or full distance, a coach or partner calling scenarios.
      2. Coach calls out a game situation before each pitch: "Runner on third, two out" or "3-2 count, bases loaded."
      3. Pitcher throws without telling you pitch type or location. You block, receive, and frame based entirely on what you see.
      4. After each sequence (3–5 pitches), reset and coach calls a new situation.
      5. Complete 4–5 scenario rounds per session. Debrief on any blocking errors or framing misses after each round.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Treat every pitch as game speed — no relaxed receives
      - [ ] Verbalize your read when appropriate ("blocking," "I got it") to build game habits
      - [ ] Review video of framing decisions after each session
      - [ ] Note which game situations create decision anxiety — those are your training targets
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Pop-Time Under Fatigue">
      Tests your throw-down mechanics after physical stress to simulate late-inning performance.

      **Steps**
      1. Complete 10 minutes of moderate conditioning first: 5 sets of 10 squats plus 5 shuffle-to-block reps left and right.
      2. Without rest, set up behind the plate for 6 pop-time throw-downs.
      3. Record each pop time. Compare to your rested baseline.
      4. Your goal: stay within 0.1 seconds of your rested pop time on all 6 reps.
      5. Log your results. A significant drop tells you exactly where your conditioning needs work.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Complete the full conditioning block — shortcuts invalidate the test
      - [ ] Mechanics under fatigue must match mechanics when rested
      - [ ] If footwork breaks down, stop and note it — that's the weak link
      - [ ] Cool down with light catching stretches and hip flexor work immediately after
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Full-Game Simulation">
      A complete simulated game behind the plate — the highest-demand session available to a catcher.

      **Steps**
      1. Set up a full pitching session with your battery mate — 50–75 pitches total, simulating a 5–7 inning start.
      2. Have a coach or partner call ball/strike on each pitch to create real feedback.
      3. Incorporate all skills in sequence: framing every borderline pitch, blocking every ball in the dirt, calling throw-down attempts on 3–4 pitches.
      4. Between innings (every 10–12 pitches), stand up, shake out your legs, and reset mentally.
      5. After the session, rate yourself on blocking, framing, and throwing separately on a 1–10 scale.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Stay fully engaged mentally on every pitch — full-game simulation is as much mental as physical
      - [ ] Do not skip the inning breaks — they replicate real game rhythm
      - [ ] Review your self-rating with video to check if your perception matches reality
      - [ ] This is your hardest session of the week — schedule it accordingly
    </Accordion>

    <Accordion title="Recovery and Mobility Routine for Catchers">
      A dedicated mobility and recovery circuit designed specifically for the demands placed on catchers.

      **Steps**
      1. **Hip Flexor Stretch (2 min):** Low lunge on each side, 60 seconds per leg.
      2. **Deep Squat Hold (2 min):** Crouch into a deep squat (heels on the ground), hands in front for balance. Hold 30 seconds, stand, repeat 4 times.
      3. **Hip 90/90 Stretch (2 min):** Sit on the floor with both legs bent at 90 degrees in front and to the side. Hinge forward over the front leg. Switch sides.
      4. **Ankle Circles (1 min):** Seated, 10 circles each direction per ankle.
      5. **Thoracic Rotation (2 min):** Seated cross-legged, place one hand behind your head and rotate your elbow toward the ceiling. 10 reps each side.
      6. **Quad Foam Roll (2 min):** If available, foam roll each quad for 30–60 seconds.

      **Checklist**
      - [ ] Do this routine immediately post-session while your body is warm
      - [ ] Never skip hip flexor work — catchers develop chronic tightness here
      - [ ] Three times per week minimum — daily is better during heavy training periods
      - [ ] Note any recurring tightness or soreness and flag it with your coach or trainer
    </Accordion>
  </Tab>
