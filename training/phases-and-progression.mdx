Player Forged organizes every training program into **phases** — structured blocks of drills that build on each other in a clear sequence. Each skill level has exactly two phases. Phase 1 lays the foundation; Phase 2 applies, intensifies, or extends what you built. You don't move between phases on a calendar — you move when you demonstrate you're ready.

## How phases work

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enter Phase 1">
    When you select a Training Focus and Skill Level for the first time, you start in **Phase 1** automatically. The phase banner shows which phase you're in at the top of your drill list.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Do each drill at its listed frequency">
    Every drill in the phase has a **weekly frequency** (for example, "3×/week" or "4×/week"). This is a recurring commitment — not a one-time event. Do the drill that many times every week until you hit the milestone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Track your completions">
    After each session, complete the **self-review checklist** for the drills you worked on. Your logged completions are what count toward the milestone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Hit the milestone">
    Each phase has a **'Move on when'** milestone — a specific performance or consistency standard you have to meet before advancing. The milestone is listed directly beneath the phase title in the app.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Advance to Phase 2">
    When you meet the milestone, the app prompts you to move to Phase 2. Tap **Advance** to unlock the next block of drills. Your Phase 1 history is preserved.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete Phase 2">
    Phase 2 has its own milestone. When you reach it, you've completed a full cycle of that focus at that skill level. From there, you can cycle back through, move up a skill level, or explore a new focus.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Phase 1: Building the foundation

Phase 1 is not "easy mode." It is foundational, and foundations must be solid. Across all seven focuses and all four skill levels, Phase 1 drills are designed to:

- **Establish the core movement patterns** for that position and tier
- **Build consistent mechanics** before adding speed, resistance, or constraint
- **Set a baseline** that Phase 2 can build on with confidence

<Note>
Phase 1 frequency is intentionally moderate. This is by design — you are building a motor pattern, and motor patterns need repetition over time, not brutal single-session volume. Trust the frequency.
</Note>

## Phase 2: Applying and intensifying

Phase 2 assumes Phase 1 is done — not just attempted, but genuinely internalized. Phase 2 drills typically:

- **Add load, speed, or constraint** to the same patterns from Phase 1
- **Extend volume** — more sets, more reps, or higher weekly frequency
- **Introduce performance benchmarks** — targets like pop time, velocity intent, or sprint time depending on your focus
- **Unlock more nuanced feedback prompts** in the self-review checklist

<Tip>
If your technique starts slipping in Phase 2 — if your mechanics feel inconsistent, your coach flags something, or your checklist self-grades are dropping — go back to Phase 1. There is no shame in it. A Phase 1 reset is not failure; it is how real athletes build durable skill.
</Tip>

## Milestones: What "Move on when" means

Every phase ends with a **'Move on when'** milestone. This is the standard you have to meet before the app considers the phase complete. Examples of what milestones look like:

<Accordion title="Consistency-based milestone (common in Beginner & Developing)">
"Move on when: You can complete all three Phase 1 drills at the listed frequency for three consecutive weeks with checklist scores of 80% or higher."

This type of milestone rewards sustained repetition and self-honest evaluation. Two good weeks and one rough week resets the clock.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Technique-based milestone (common in Intermediate)">
"Move on when: Your Video Review checklist shows the correct hip-to-shoulder separation checkpoint on 8 out of 10 recorded reps."

This type of milestone is tied directly to a specific mechanical standard visible in your recorded footage. It requires the Video Review tool and honest self-assessment.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Performance-based milestone (common in Advanced)">
"Move on when: You hit your target sprint time on the 60-yard dash standard for your focus, confirmed across at least two separate sessions."

This type of milestone is output-based. You either hit the number or you don't. Keep training until you do.
</Accordion>

## Timeline: Expect 2–6 weeks per phase

There is no fixed schedule. Some athletes blow through Phase 1 in two weeks because they enter with strong existing mechanics and just need the structure. Others spend six weeks in Phase 1 because they're building something real from scratch. **Both of those outcomes are correct.**

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Faster progressions" icon="forward">
    If you're advancing in under 3 weeks, that's fine — it likely means you selected the right skill level and are consistent. Consider whether moving up a skill tier makes sense after the full cycle.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Slower progressions" icon="hourglass">
    If you're in Phase 1 for 6+ weeks, that's also fine — it means the milestone is doing its job. Don't inflate your checklist scores to force advancement. The milestone protects you.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Phases are recurring, not one-time events

Completing both phases doesn't retire the program — it gives you a full cycle to repeat. Many athletes cycle through the same focus and level multiple times in a season, especially during an off-season block. Each new cycle builds on the previous one. You'll move through Phase 1 faster on your second cycle, and Phase 2 will feel more manageable because the patterns are already there.

<Note>
Repeating a full phase cycle is one of the most effective things you can do. Elite players repeat fundamentals relentlessly — not because they forgot them, but because repetition is how fundamentals become automatic under pressure.
</Note>

## Coach-assigned training and the banner

When your coach assigns a specific training focus and skill level for you, a banner appears at the top of your Training tab that reads **"Assigned by Coach."** Tapping this banner does two things:

1. **Auto-navigates** you directly to the correct Training Focus and Skill Level — no manual searching
2. **Shows the coach's note** (if your coach added one) explaining what to focus on during that session

<Tip>
Coach-assigned training overrides nothing — your personal phase progress and drill history are unaffected. The banner is simply a smart shortcut to the program your coach selected for you.
</Tip>

<Warning>
If a coach assigns a focus and skill level that doesn't match where you currently are in your program, don't manually jump phases to match. Work through the assigned program as given, and talk to your coach if something feels misaligned.
</Warning>

## Frequently asked questions

<Accordion title="What happens if I skip a week?">
Nothing is reset automatically. The milestone clock doesn't count calendar days — it counts demonstrated completions and checklist performance. Pick up where you left off. If the skip was long (2+ weeks), your mechanics may feel rusty, so consider treating your first session back as a light re-entry before pushing full intensity.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I work on both phases at once?">
No. Phase 2 is locked until you advance from Phase 1. This is intentional — Phase 2 is designed for athletes who have the Phase 1 foundation, and skipping it increases the risk of building on unstable mechanics.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I work on Phase 1 of one skill level and Phase 2 of another?">
Yes. Your phase progress is tracked independently per focus and per skill level. You might be in Phase 2 of Intermediate Infielder and Phase 1 of Intermediate Speed & Conditioning at the same time.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What does 'cycling back' through phases look like?">
After completing Phase 2, the app gives you the option to restart at Phase 1. Your previous milestone history is preserved as a log. You'll typically find that your second pass through Phase 1 is faster — and that Phase 2 asks more of you once your baseline is higher.
</Accordion>
