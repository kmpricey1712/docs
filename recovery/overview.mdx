Rest isn't the absence of training — it's a required part of it. The **Rest & Off-Season** tab exists because the most common reason young baseball players plateau or get hurt isn't that they trained too little. It's that they never actually stopped. Player Forged treats recovery as a structured program, not a blank space on the calendar, so you know what you should be doing whether it's the middle of the season or the middle of January.

<Note>
  **This is general guidance, not medical advice.** The recommendations on these pages are designed to support healthy development for youth and high school baseball players as a population. They are not a substitute for evaluation by a doctor, physical therapist, or certified athletic trainer. If you or your athlete is experiencing pain — especially in the shoulder, elbow, or any joint — stop throwing, rest, and consult a qualified medical professional. Never push through joint pain.
</Note>

## How the tab works

The Rest & Off-Season tab is organized the same way the rest of Player Forged is: you select a **Skill Level** to see guidance matched to that stage of development. The same four tiers used throughout the app apply here.

| Skill Level | Who it's for |
|---|---|
| **Beginner** | Ages ~7–10, first 1–2 years of organized baseball |
| **Developing** | Ages ~11–14, building foundational mechanics |
| **Intermediate** | Ages ~14–16, competitive travel or high school ball |
| **Advanced** | Ages ~16–18+, high school varsity and beyond |

<Tip>
  If an athlete plays up a level competitively but is younger or less physically mature, use the recovery guidance for their actual age range rather than their competitive level. Physical development doesn't always match skill development.
</Tip>

## Two sections

The Recovery tab contains two main sections:

### Rest Guidance

In-season habits for staying healthy while the season is active. This section covers rest days per week, sleep targets, warning signs to watch for, and how to think about arm care during the competitive calendar. See [Rest Guidance](/recovery/rest-guidance) for the full breakdown by skill level.

### Off-Season Program

A phased block program for the period between seasons. The off-season is where long-term development actually happens — it's when you build the physical foundation that makes in-season training pay off. Each skill level has its own phase structure with specific weekly schedules. See [Off-Season Program](/recovery/off-season-program) for the full program.

## Why this matters more than most players think

Throwing a baseball is one of the most violent, repetitive motions in all of sport. The shoulder and elbow absorb enormous stress on every pitch, and that stress accumulates. Youth and adolescent athletes are especially vulnerable because bones, growth plates, and connective tissue are still developing — structures that haven't finished growing can't handle the same load as a mature adult body.

The players who stay healthy and keep improving through high school and beyond are rarely the ones who threw the most year-round. They're the ones who trained smart, rested deliberately, and treated the off-season as an opportunity instead of a problem to fill.

<Accordion title="What counts as a 'rest day' in baseball?">
  A rest day means no throwing — not a lighter throwing day, not long toss at 70%, not bullpen work. For pitchers especially, even moderate throwing keeps the arm under load and prevents the tissue repair that rest days are supposed to allow. A rest day can still include lower-body conditioning, mobility work, or non-throwing strength training. But the arm needs to come off completely.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do I use the Recovery tab alongside the main training drills?">
  The Rest & Off-Season tab supplements the core drill program — it doesn't replace it. During the season, use rest guidance to structure your week around training sessions. In the off-season, the phased program replaces your normal drill schedule for most of the break, then transitions back into baseball-specific work as pre-season approaches.
</Accordion>
