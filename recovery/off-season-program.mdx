---
title: "Baseball Off-Season Training Program by Skill Level"
sidebarTitle: "Off-Season Program"
description: "Phased off-season programs by skill level — structured blocks that build your physical foundation so you return to spring stronger than you left."
---

The off-season is the most underused asset in a baseball player's development. While in-season training is about performing and maintaining, the off-season is when you actually build — physical capacity, arm strength, mobility, speed, and the structural resilience that lets you handle a full season without breaking down. Every skill level has a phased program below. The phases aren't suggestions — they exist in a specific order because skipping the early work undermines everything that comes after it.

<Note>
  **This is general guidance, not medical advice.** These programs are designed as general frameworks for healthy youth and high school baseball players. They are not a substitute for individualized advice from a qualified strength and conditioning coach, physical therapist, or sports medicine professional. If you're managing an injury or returning from one, consult a medical professional before following any off-season program.
</Note>

  <Tab title="Beginner">
    ## Beginner Off-Season Program (~17–18 weeks)

    Beginner athletes don't need a structured strength program — they need rest, variety, and a chance to be kids who happen to play baseball. The off-season at this level is mostly about letting the body recover fully and keeping athleticism broad.

    ---

    ### Phase 1 — Shut It Down (Weeks 1–6)

    No baseball. That's it. Six weeks of completely stepping away from the sport. This isn't laziness — it's what the arm and the whole body needs after a season of repetitive motion.

    **Goals:** Full recovery, give the arm a genuine break, do whatever is fun.

    **What to do:** Play outside, ride bikes, swim, shoot hoops — any unstructured physical activity that isn't baseball. There should be zero throwing in this block.

    | Day | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | Monday | Free play / any other sport |
    | Tuesday | Rest or light activity |
    | Wednesday | Free play |
    | Thursday | Rest |
    | Friday | Free play / any other sport |
    | Saturday | Free or family activity |
    | Sunday | Full rest |

    <Note>
      Six weeks with no baseball will feel like a long time to a motivated young athlete. That's fine. The arm is still recovering even when it doesn't feel sore.
    </Note>

    ---

    ### Phase 2 — Play Other Sports (Weeks 7–14)

    Eight weeks of participating in a different organized sport, pickup games, or recreational activity **2–3 times per week**. The goal is developing general athleticism — coordination, lateral movement, explosiveness, and body awareness — using a totally different set of movement patterns.

    **Goals:** Broad athletic development, keep the body active, continue giving the arm a break.

    **Good options:** Soccer, basketball, flag football, swimming, wrestling, martial arts. Anything that moves the body differently than baseball does.

    | Day | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | Monday | Other sport / recreational activity |
    | Tuesday | Rest |
    | Wednesday | Other sport / recreational activity |
    | Thursday | Rest |
    | Friday | Other sport (optional 3rd session) |
    | Saturday | Free play |
    | Sunday | Rest |

    <Tip>
      There's no need to be formal about this phase for young athletes. If they're in a recreational basketball league or playing pickup soccer with friends three times a week, that counts. The point is variety and movement — not structured training.
    </Tip>

    ---

    ### Phase 3 — Ease Back In (Weeks 15–17/18)

    Light baseball returns for the final 3–4 weeks before pre-season. The emphasis is on easy, comfortable movement — not preparing for tryouts, not working on mechanics, just getting the arm moving again and reminding the body what baseball feels like.

    **Goals:** Reintroduce throwing gently, rebuild feel for the ball, prepare for structured pre-season.

    **Schedule:** Easy catch play **2 times per week** + **tee work 1–2 times per week**. No competitive throwing, no bullpens, no distance work.

    | Day | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | Monday | Easy catch (20–30 throws at short distance) |
    | Tuesday | Rest |
    | Wednesday | Tee work (15–20 swings, focus on feel) |
    | Thursday | Rest |
    | Friday | Easy catch (20–30 throws) |
    | Saturday | Tee work or rest |
    | Sunday | Rest |

    <Warning>
      "Ease back in" means ease back in. If an athlete tries to ramp up intensity in these final weeks because they're excited for the season, they're short-circuiting the recovery the earlier phases built. Keep it light.
    </Warning>

  <Tab title="Developing">
    ## Developing Off-Season Program (~16 weeks)

    Developing athletes are at the stage where the off-season starts to have real training value. This program introduces structured physical preparation for the first time — but the order matters. The body needs to recover before it can build.

    ---

    ### Phase 1 — Active Rest (Weeks 1–4)

    **Zero throwing.** The arm gets a complete break while the rest of the body starts gentle movement work.

    **Goals:** Full arm recovery, re-establish baseline movement quality, light conditioning.

    **What to do:** Bodyweight circuits 3x/week — think push-ups, bodyweight squats, lunges, planks, and mobility work. Nothing with a barbell, nothing heavy. The goal is to move well, not to stress the system.

    | Day | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | Monday | Bodyweight circuit (20–25 min) |
    | Tuesday | Rest or light walking |
    | Wednesday | Mobility & flexibility work (15–20 min) |
    | Thursday | Rest |
    | Friday | Bodyweight circuit (20–25 min) |
    | Saturday | Light activity (swimming, hiking, casual sport) |
    | Sunday | Full rest |

    **Sample bodyweight circuit:**
    | Exercise | Sets | Reps |
    |---|---|---|
    | Push-ups | 3 | 10–15 |
    | Bodyweight squat | 3 | 15 |
    | Reverse lunge | 3 | 10 each leg |
    | Plank | 3 | 20–30 sec |
    | Hip hinge (bodyweight RDL) | 3 | 12 |
    | Band pull-apart | 3 | 15 |

    ---

    ### Phase 2 — Build the Base (Weeks 5–10)

    Strength and speed work begins in earnest. Throwing returns — but only **once per week**, with low volume and zero intensity pressure.

    **Goals:** Build general strength and speed, reintroduce the arm gently, establish physical capacity for the baseball-specific block ahead.

    **Strength focus:** Lower-body power (squats, deadlifts if appropriate), upper-body push/pull balance, core stability. Two to three sessions per week.

    **Throwing:** Once per week, easy catch at moderate distance. Focus is on feel and mechanics, not velocity.

    | Day | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | Monday | Lower-body strength (45–50 min) |
    | Tuesday | Easy throwing session (30–40 throws) |
    | Wednesday | Rest |
    | Thursday | Upper-body strength (45 min) |
    | Friday | Speed/agility work (ladder, shuttle runs, broad jumps) |
    | Saturday | Rest |
    | Sunday | Full rest |

    <Tip>
      The single weekly throwing session in this phase isn't about arm development — it's about keeping the mechanics sharp while the body builds strength. Resist the urge to add a second session until Phase 3.
    </Tip>

    ---

    ### Phase 3 — Baseball Ramps Back (Weeks 11–16)

    Throwing volume increases gradually over six weeks. Strength work continues but shifts to support baseball movement patterns.

    **Goals:** Return to full throwing capacity, convert general strength to baseball-specific power, prepare for pre-season training.

    **Throwing progression:** Start the block at 2 sessions per week, build to 3 by week 14. Increase volume by no more than 10% per week. Add bullpen sessions for pitchers in the final 2 weeks.

    | Day | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | Monday | Strength (baseball-focused — rotational core, hip work) |
    | Tuesday | Throwing session (light bullpen or long toss, moderate volume) |
    | Wednesday | Speed/conditioning |
    | Thursday | Rest or mobility |
    | Friday | Throwing session (increasing volume week over week) |
    | Saturday | Optional light session or scrimmage (weeks 14–16) |
    | Sunday | Full rest |

    <Warning>
      Gradual is the operative word. Going from once-a-week throwing in Phase 2 to three full bullpens per week in the first week of Phase 3 is how arm injuries happen at the start of pre-season. Use the 10% weekly volume rule and take it seriously.
    </Warning>

  <Tab title="Intermediate">
    ## Intermediate Off-Season Program (~19–20 weeks)

    Intermediate athletes are ready for a real off-season program — four structured phases that move from recovery through strength, baseball-specific conversion, and pre-season taper. This is a serious block of training, and the work you put in here directly determines your ceiling during the season.

    ---

    ### Phase 1 — Recovery Block (Weeks 1–4)

    No throwing. Light strength work only.

    **Goals:** Full arm recovery, maintain physical base, decompress from the season.

    **Strength:** Light — 2 sessions per week of full-body work at 50–60% of your normal training intensity. Focus on movement quality, not load. Include shoulder health exercises (face pulls, band pull-aparts, external rotation work) every session.

    | Day | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | Monday | Light full-body strength (50–60% intensity) |
    | Tuesday | Mobility & flexibility (hip flexors, thoracic spine, shoulder) |
    | Wednesday | Rest or light cardio (bike, swim, jog) |
    | Thursday | Light full-body strength |
    | Friday | Mobility or light conditioning |
    | Saturday | Non-baseball recreational activity |
    | Sunday | Full rest |

    **Shoulder health work (do this every strength session):**
    | Exercise | Sets | Reps |
    |---|---|---|
    | Band pull-apart | 3 | 15–20 |
    | Face pull | 3 | 15 |
    | Side-lying external rotation | 3 | 12 each |
    | Prone Y/T/W | 2 | 10 each |

    ---

    ### Phase 2 — Strength & Speed Block (Weeks 5–10)

    The meat of the off-season program. Six weeks of building a genuine physical base — the foundation that everything baseball-specific runs on.

    **Goals:** Increase lower-body strength, develop hip power and explosiveness, establish upper-body pulling strength, build sprint capacity.

    **Throwing:** Not yet — or minimal (once a week at low intensity for pitchers managing arm health). Strength is the priority.

    | Day | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | Monday | Lower-body strength (squat/hinge focus) |
    | Tuesday | Speed & agility (sprints, change of direction) |
    | Wednesday | Upper-body strength (pull emphasis — rows, pull-ups, rear delt work) |
    | Thursday | Active recovery (light mobility, walking, foam rolling) |
    | Friday | Lower-body power (jumps, single-leg work, trap bar deadlift) |
    | Saturday | Upper-body push/pull + core (rotational work) |
    | Sunday | Full rest |

    **Sample lower-body day:**
    | Exercise | Sets | Reps |
    |---|---|---|
    | Back squat or goblet squat | 4 | 5–6 |
    | Romanian deadlift | 3 | 8 |
    | Bulgarian split squat | 3 | 8 each |
    | Hip thrust | 3 | 10 |
    | Box jump | 3 | 5 |
    | Copenhagen plank | 3 | 20 sec each |

    ---

    ### Phase 3 — Baseball-Specific Block (Weeks 11–16)

    Strength converts to baseball power. Throwing returns at full volume and intensity ramps progressively.

    **Goals:** Connect off-season strength to on-field performance, rebuild throwing arm capacity, sharpen mechanics.

    **Throwing:** 3 sessions per week. Begin the block with moderate volume and build. Pitchers add bullpens starting week 13–14. Position players work on throwing mechanics and arm accuracy.

    | Day | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | Monday | Rotational power + lower-body maintenance |
    | Tuesday | Throwing session (mechanics + increasing volume) |
    | Wednesday | Upper-body strength (maintain, don't add load) + shoulder health |
    | Thursday | Throwing session (bullpen or long toss) |
    | Friday | Speed, agility, and base-running movement patterns |
    | Saturday | Throwing session + hitting (tee, soft toss, front toss) |
    | Sunday | Rest |

    ---

    ### Phase 4 — Pre-Season Ramp (Weeks 17–19/20)

    Volume tapers, intensity stays up. The goal is to arrive at the first week of pre-season feeling sharp, not tired.

    **Goals:** Peak readiness, reduce accumulated fatigue, maintain mechanics under moderate load.

    **What changes:** Cut total weekly throwing volume by 20–30% compared to Phase 3, but keep effort levels in the sessions you do have. Reduce strength training to maintenance — 2 sessions per week, moderate weight, full range of motion.

    | Day | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | Monday | Maintenance strength (2 sets per exercise, moderate load) |
    | Tuesday | Throwing session (full intensity, moderate volume) |
    | Wednesday | Mobility + shoulder health |
    | Thursday | Hitting + light throwing |
    | Friday | Throwing session (quality over quantity) |
    | Saturday | Optional scrimmage or batting practice |
    | Sunday | Full rest |

    <Tip>
      Arriving at pre-season slightly under-loaded is far better than arriving over-loaded. You can build intensity quickly once the season starts. You can't easily recover from fatigue you accumulated trying to peak too early.
    </Tip>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Advanced">
    ## Advanced Off-Season Program (~21–22 weeks)

    Advanced athletes need a professional-grade approach to the off-season. Four phases — including a meaningful assessment period, a true max-strength block, a power and velocity development phase, and a pre-season taper — give you the full development arc to come back better than you left.

    ---

    ### Phase 1 — Shutdown & Assess (Weeks 1–4)

    The season just ended. Shut the arm down completely and use this time productively.

    **Goals:** Full physical recovery, honest review of the past season, set specific targets for the off-season.

    **The assessment piece is real training.** Watch film from the past season. Identify 1–2 specific things — mechanics, pitch mix, defensive reads, bat path — you want to change. Write them down. These become the targets for Phase 3 and 4.

    **Strength:** Light, 2x/week. Movement quality only — no heavy lifting, no intensity. Keep shoulder health work running throughout.

    | Day | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | Monday | Film review + light mobility work |
    | Tuesday | Light full-body strength (movement quality focus) |
    | Wednesday | Rest or light cardio |
    | Thursday | Light full-body strength + shoulder health |
    | Friday | Non-baseball activity (any other sport or recreation) |
    | Saturday | Film review / planning / rest |
    | Sunday | Full rest |

    <Accordion title="How to do a useful film review">
      Pull 3–5 games or outings from across the season — early, middle, and late. Look for consistent patterns, not one-off plays. For pitchers: mechanics at peak fatigue vs. fresh mechanics, pitch command trends, velocity across a start. For hitters: approach on first-pitch strikes, two-strike adjustments, pull vs. middle-away tendencies. Pick the 1–2 most consistent fixable issues — not 6, not 10. You can only meaningfully work on 1–2 things at a time.
    </Accordion>

    ---

    ### Phase 2 — Max Strength Block (Weeks 5–11)

    Seven weeks of serious lifting. This is the highest training load of the off-season program, and it's designed that way on purpose. Strength is the base that power, velocity, and durability all run on.

    **Goals:** Build maximum strength in lower body, posterior chain, and upper back. Develop the structural foundation for velocity and power work in Phase 3.

    **Deload:** Take a deliberate deload week every 4th week. Week 8 is your deload — cut volume by 40%, keep intensity moderate.

    **Throwing:** None, or minimal (once a week for arm health). The arm is still recovering and the focus is the weight room.

    | Day | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | Monday | Lower-body max strength (squat / deadlift focus) |
    | Tuesday | Upper-body pulling (weighted pull-ups, barbell row, face pulls) |
    | Wednesday | Sprint work + hip mobility |
    | Thursday | Lower-body accessory (single-leg, hip thrust, glute work) |
    | Friday | Upper-body push + rotational core |
    | Saturday | Optional: conditioning or make-up session |
    | Sunday | Full rest |

    **Sample lower-body max strength day:**
    | Exercise | Sets | Reps | Notes |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Back squat | 5 | 3–5 | Work up to a top set |
    | Romanian deadlift | 4 | 5 | Heavy but controlled |
    | Bulgarian split squat | 3 | 6 each | Load progressively |
    | Hip thrust | 4 | 8 | Drive through glutes |
    | Broad jump | 3 | 5 | Explosive, full reset each rep |
    | Copenhagen plank | 3 | 25 sec each | — |

    **Deload week (Week 8) structure:**
    | Day | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | Monday | Lower body — 60% normal volume, 70% intensity |
    | Tuesday | Upper body — same reduction |
    | Wednesday | Easy cardio (30 min bike or jog) |
    | Thursday | Mobility & flexibility session |
    | Friday | Light full-body (bodyweight or very light load) |
    | Saturday–Sunday | Rest |

    ---

    ### Phase 3 — Power & Velocity Block (Weeks 12–18)

    Strength converts to speed. This is where velocity development happens for pitchers and exit velocity development happens for hitters. The weight room shifts from heavy and slow to explosive. Throwing returns at full volume.

    **Goals:** Translate off-season strength into on-field power, develop arm speed, work on the 1–2 targets identified in Phase 1 assessment.

    **Long toss:** Integrated into the throwing program 2x/week. Follow a progressive distance program — don't jump straight to max distance.

    **Recorded sessions:** Record at least one throwing or hitting session per week. Compare against your Phase 1 film to track progress on your target areas.

    | Day | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | Monday | Lower-body power (jump variations, trap bar jumps, med ball work) |
    | Tuesday | Throwing session (long toss — progressive distance program) |
    | Wednesday | Upper-body explosive (med ball chest pass, rotational throws, explosive rows) |
    | Thursday | Throwing session (bullpen or batting practice — recorded) |
    | Friday | Sprint & change-of-direction work |
    | Saturday | Throwing session + game-situation work |
    | Sunday | Rest |

    **Long toss distance progression (pitchers):**
    | Week | Target Distance | Pulldown Work |
    |---|---|---|
    | 12–13 | 90–120 ft | No |
    | 14–15 | 120–150 ft | Light |
    | 16 | 150–180 ft | Moderate |
    | 17–18 | Max distance + pulldowns | Yes |

    <Note>
      "Recorded sessions" doesn't require a Rapsodo or TrackMan. A phone on a tripod at 60fps captures enough to review mechanics. The habit of watching yourself throw or hit — and comparing it against a specific target — is what matters.
    </Note>

    ---

    ### Phase 4 — Pre-Season Taper (Weeks 19–21/22)

    Volume drops. Intensity stays. The goal is to arrive sharp.

    **Goals:** Peak readiness, eliminate accumulated fatigue, nail down the mechanical changes from Phase 3, prepare for the first practice.

    **What changes:**
    - Throwing volume drops 25–35% from Phase 3 peak
    - Strength training goes to maintenance (2 sessions, 2–3 sets per movement)
    - Intensity in throwing and hitting sessions stays high — you're not "taking it easy," you're being precise about what you do

    | Day | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | Monday | Maintenance strength (2 sets per exercise, moderate load) |
    | Tuesday | Throwing session (full intent, 60–70% of peak volume) |
    | Wednesday | Mobility + shoulder health + light conditioning |
    | Thursday | Hitting + short throwing |
    | Friday | Throwing session (bullpen or game-simulated work) |
    | Saturday | Optional scrimmage or live at-bats |
    | Sunday | Full rest |

    <Tip>
      If you feel unusually fresh in weeks 20–21, that's the program working — not a sign you should add volume back. Freshness going into pre-season is the goal. Trust the taper.
    </Tip>

    <Warning>
      Advanced athletes who skip or shorten the taper often show up to pre-season carrying fatigue from the power block. The first two weeks of pre-season feel fine because adrenaline masks it. By week four it catches up. Protect the taper.
    </Warning>
